# Changelog

What changes in Eden from one version to the next, newest first.

**Unreleased** gathers what is done since the last version, as it lands:
every fix and every addition gets its line the day it is merged. When a
version ships, the section takes its number and date, and its gist becomes
the paragraph in `NOTES.md`. What is planned but not done yet lives in
[ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md).

## Unreleased

- Tests that passed alone no longer fail when run with others: the download tests let go of their window and its browser, which, left alive, broke the column's drag tests run after them; a pinned square's test waits for its carry to be called off rather than a fixed moment; and on iPhone, each way of closing a pin is tested over a folder of its own (#16).
- A page closed on another device from its row, or sent there, stays as it was last asked, on Mac and iPhone: an ask from a Mac that was offline meanwhile, arriving after a newer one was done, no longer undoes it, and which ask is newer no longer depends on whose clock runs ahead. Each ask now says which of the other device's tabs it was made from, by that device's own clock (#13).
- Test runs on the Mac no longer leave their throwaway folders and settings piling up in Library: each run's go when it ends, and whatever an earlier run left, one that crashed included or settings written on its way out, goes when the next one starts (#7).
- On the Mac, a pinned row put down shows a × where its minus was: a click takes the pin out, as Delete in its menu does. ⇧⌘T brings a pin taken out either way back as a pin, with its letter, its page and its name, at its place among the pins and in its folder if that is still there; taken out asleep, it comes back asleep and you stay where you are.
- On the Mac, a pinned row that has gone on from its page says so: its pinned page's icon stays at its start, then a slash, then the title of the page it is on now, and a click on that icon takes it back; the slash opens and closes with the title, which keeps its place as it changes. A page its address sends it to as it loads, such as a mail app's inbox, still counts as home, for Back to Pinned Page and ⌘-click too, and a pin there is no longer offered to your other devices as an open page. A pin away from its page when Eden quits, or put down there, comes back by its page's title rather than the site's address. Every tab's title holds while the next page loads, rather than showing the address for a moment before the new title.
- On the Mac, the line above your tabs shows whenever something is kept above it, pinned squares alone included, even with no tab under it; it still goes with the last pin and the last of your other devices' groups.
- The newest ask for a page on another device wins, on Mac and iPhone: a page sent there and then closed from its row stays closed, rather than coming back after each press of the cross, and a page closed and then sent again opens; the older asks for that page go with the newest. A page closed or sent is counted done once that device's own tabs are on disk, whatever else failed to be written meanwhile, and what happens to asks, and every file Eden couldn't write, is now in the device's log.
- On the Mac, pins are where pages are kept: pinned rows, folders in them and pins keeping their site are always on, and their switches have left Settings (the one-click setup in Spaces & Pins now sets only the tabs on the left and Spaces). Bookmarks have left the Mac: the bookmarks bar and its setting, the Bookmarks menu, the bookmark button and its card, the bookmarks page and the bookmark rows in the address field are gone, and the pages you keep come up in the field as the pinned tabs they are, asleep or in a folded folder too. ⌘D pins the page in front as a row, or unpins it (Duplicate Tab has no key until you give it one). A folder of pinned rows is a tab group in all but where it is kept: the same tinted surface, heading and fold, a colour and an icon of its own from the same menu (until one is chosen, a folder whose front tilts open as the folder unfolds), the same card when the pointer rests on its heading, and like a group it holds pages, not other folders; folders you had inside others are brought up once, their pages into the folder at the top they were in. Folders stand among the pinned rows where they are put, not all above them, and Delete Folder keeps its pins, where the folder stood. The bookmarks you have become pinned rows at the top of the rows in your first space, each of their folders a folder of pinned rows of the same name with the pages of the folders inside it in it, in order, and so do the ones your iPhone adds later, each only once: a page you unpin doesn't come back. The bookmarks file stays as a backup and still syncs with your iPhone, which keeps its Bookmarks sheet. Bringing things over from another browser brings its bookmarks in as pinned rows in a folder named after it and Bookmarks (a bookmarks file's after the file), every page in that one folder, and Replace takes back out exactly those. Extensions that use bookmarks read and change your pinned rows and their folders, and are told a folder can't go inside another. One line divides what is kept from your tabs: your other devices' groups stand above it with the pins, Clear is on it, and the New tab row comes straight under it when new tabs go to the top, with no second line under that.
- One carry for the whole of the Mac's column: a tab carried over the pinned rows makes them part where it would land, as your tabs do under the line, with the same spring and the same gap, where a blue line used to show the place; let go, it is pinned there, at the top of the rows or in a folder at any place in it, and a folder springs open under a tab resting on it. A tab group carried up over the line by its heading parts the pinned rows for the whole group and becomes a folder there, its tabs pinned in it, and a folder carried down becomes a tab group again, its pins tabs, each with the place it leaves closing behind it; groups and folders carried among their own make way as they go. A pin shown in every space, carried from the squares into the rows or back, stays in every space, among the rows every space shows at the head of the rows. A group never goes inside another, and a private tab or a split pair can't be pinned, so nothing parts for them above the line. Folders are drawn as tab groups are in every way but their folder icon and their missing close button, a pinned row put down in a folder dims as a tab put away in a group does, Fold All Groups folds the folders too, and ⌘1–9 count a folded folder once, as a folded group.
- Resting the pointer on a tab in the Mac's column, a row, a pinned square or a tile of the icon column, brings up a card beside the column: a picture of its page, its title and its site. Its buttons copy the address, duplicate the tab and pin or unpin it; while the tab plays, or was paused from Eden, they also pause or play it and mute it. The card comes after half a second and then follows the pointer from tab to tab at once: one card that glides to the next tab on a spring and takes its height, its content trailing at a quarter of its pace, the old fading out and the new in, rather than one card fading for another. Heading over to it, the pointer may cross other tabs without the card turning to them, as a menu waits for the pointer on its way into a submenu. A click, a scroll or a key puts it away. It takes the place of a square's and a tile's tooltip. A group's heading has one too, folded or not: the four of its tabs you were in last as one picture (whole, side by side, in three columns, or in the corners), each part going to its tab, with up to three of their icons and how many more on a chip edged in the group's colour, its name and how many tabs it holds, and buttons to copy all its links, duplicate it, fold or unfold it, and mute or unmute it while a tab in it plays.
- Pictures of pages are kept on disk, on the Mac as on iPhone, so a tab brought back after Eden quits shows its page on its hover card, or its card in the tab overview, as you last left it. A private tab's never is. Each space's or profile's sign-ins keep their own pictures, Clear History takes them all, and one not seen for a month goes. The iPhone's pictures kept before, one a page whatever the space, are let go of once, and its cards fetch new ones as they always have.
- ⌘T's dialog does what ⌘K's switcher did, and more: ⌘K and Search Tabs… are gone, and ⌘K is the page's again. Before anything is typed, the dialog lists, for ⌘T and under ⌘L's address alike, a link you copied in the last minute (Visit from clipboard), your open tabs, then the sites you go to most; while typing, the open tabs that answer to it come after the row Return takes, with Switch to Tab and the name of the tab group each is in, and a tab group or space you name is offered as Open. An open tab picked shows its address in the field after Switch to tab. Putting a site in the field with Tab, or taking it out, pulses the dialog, and it glows as one goes in. The dialog is glass over the page in Eden's colours (solid with Reduce Transparency), lists seven rows before it scrolls, lights the column's New tab row while it is up, and keeps what was typed for 45 seconds after it is put away. Recent places in the new tab is gone from Settings: the sites you go to most are always there.
- The new tab is a dialog: ⌘T, the + row and File › New Tab bring the field up over the page you are on, and Return makes the tab; Escape, ⌘T again or a click outside leaves you where you were. Settings no longer offers a blank new tab instead. The dialog is one panel, the field in a band across the top and what it finds below a hairline, each row the site's icon, its name and, after a dash, where it is, and an open tab offering Switch to Tab. It comes and goes at once, lifted off the page by a broad shadow rather than dimming it, in Eden's own colours, corners and type. ⌘L and ⌘K wear it too.
- One toast for everything Eden says for a moment, on Mac and iPhone: a Liquid Glass capsule that drops in at the top centre with a little bounce and slips back up. Something done wears a check that draws itself in; something gone wrong, a mark of its own. A tab closed in the iPhone's tab overview offers Reopen (on the Mac, ⇧⌘T as ever, without a toast); something hidden, Undo; a download, Show in Finder. Said once however often it is said, held while the pointer is on it, and read out by VoiceOver.
- Your pinned tiles come once across your devices: a pin at its own page is no longer listed again among another device's open tabs (one that has gone on to another page is, as that page), and a page pinned apart on your Mac and your iPhone becomes one pin everywhere, its tab carrying on as that one's, page and history with it.
- Signed in after a quit, an update or a crash, on Mac and iPhone: a site's session cookies, the ones without an expiry that a sign-in without "keep me signed in", a cart, a payment on its way or a video site's theatre mode live in, come back with your tabs. They are kept sealed with a key that never leaves the device's keychain, never from a private tab and never synced; Clear Website Data takes them, and a fresh start begins without them.
- On iPhone, reaching the end of a page leaves the controls as they are: the page springing back from its end no longer flickers them open and shut, and a second pull past the end brings them back in full. They shrink to the site's name and grow back in one smooth piece, with nothing left of back or the tabs at the name's ends, and the page no longer shifts or leaves a dark strip below it as they go.
- The iPhone's tab overview, redesigned as the iPhone's own app switcher: each tab a card of its page, its site's icon and name above it, in a deck you swipe through sideways, each in its place and the deck opening at the tab you were on, the older stacked behind it to the left. Every card shows its page: kept from last time, or loaded out of sight for it, nearest first. Only the card in the middle is named, as in iOS. Swipe a card up to close it, and Reopen brings it back for a moment; tap one and it grows back into its page, as the page shrank into it. Each space is a room in its own colour, switched at the top, and private is a dark room of its own; your other devices' pages are a tap away there, with how far you had read the one you were just on. The bar at the foot is the browser's own, turned as the page zooms: back becomes a new tab, the address field a search through the cards (Return searches the web), and the tabs button Done, each piece of glass flowing into the next.
- A playing or muted tab's speaker in the Mac's column sits just after the site's icon, there for as long as the page plays or is muted, where it was a small circle at the row's end that stepped aside for the cross; like the cross it wears its grey square only under the pointer, and a click on it mutes or unmutes without going to the tab. The player at the foot of the column, a split pair's lines and their crosses wear the same squares.
- A page closed or sent from another device's list is done even if Eden quits before it gets to it: the ask stands until its tabs have changed, and the list here shows it as asked until then rather than until the device next says its tabs. A page sent to an iPhone space with no tabs yet opens there, and half a split pair carried into a group or onto a device goes on its own, as it does onto a tab.
- Tab groups stand among your tabs on the Mac, wherever you put them, rather than all above them: carry a group by its heading along the column and the tabs and other groups make way, as they do for a tab. A tab carried past a group passes it whole, the top of a heading being the place just before its group; a new group stands where its tab was, a tab added to a group goes to its end without moving the group, and ⌘1–9, the tabs across the top and the spaces passing in a swipe all keep the same order. Clear sits over the run and still keeps the groups in it.
- Carrying a tab in the Mac's column shows where it will land, with tab groups on as without: along its own group it moves as it goes, the others making way, and over another group, or out among the tabs in none, the rows there part for it while its own place closes, so letting go puts it exactly where the gap was. Resting on a folded group opens it after a moment, as a folder springs open on the Mac, and folds it again if the tab goes elsewhere; a group keeps a tab a little past its edge, so a hand on the line between two doesn't flick between them, and a tap of the trackpad says the gap has moved into another one.
- Your other devices' tabs in the Mac's column can be closed from there: the cross under the pointer closes the page on its device the next time that device syncs, and it leaves the list here at once. An iPhone, iPad or Mac closes a page asked of it as its own cross would, a pin put down. A tab carried onto one of their groups opens there too: the group lights and opens a place at its end, the page is in its list at once, and the device opens it behind the page in front, in the space it was in here, when it next syncs; the tab stays here. A page open here already wears a small green dot carved into its icon in place of the word Open.
- The cross that closes a tab or a group in the Mac's column is a plain cross the size of the row's text, that wears a rounded grey square under the pointer, in place of the small grey circle.
- A site icon that is one grey drawn on nothing, such as a black cat, is turned over where it would all but vanish: white on a dark tile or column, black on a light one, on Mac, iPhone and iPad. Icons with colour, or a ground of their own, are left as they are.
- Pins on iPhone and iPad wear their site's icon, as on the Mac, in place of a letter: what the page declares, the best of it, kept by site, and for a pin whose page hasn't been opened yet, where sites keep one by custom. The pin in front is lit in its icon's own colour. Never fetched for a private tab, and Clear History takes them all. The pins are laid out as the Mac's column lays them out: no more than four to a row, the rows even, each spanning the width.
- A page handed off with Handoff carries on as one picked from your other devices' tabs does: to its tab if it is open already, else in its own space, as far down as you had read and in reading mode if you were, with a line saying where it came from. The Mac and iPhone send how the page is left with it. Your other devices are called by their own names: "Continued from Subhajit's iPhone".
- The iPhone's controls float over the page on glass: back, the address field with the page's menu folded into its start, and the tabs. Back stretches to hold forward beside it once there is somewhere to go, and holding either lists the pages that way; holding the tabs opens or closes one. Reading on down a page shrinks the controls to the site's name, out of the way; a look back up, a tap on the name, or another page brings them back. The page runs up under the status bar too, its first screen starting just below it, and the clock and status icons are dark over a light page and light over a dark one, whatever the phone's appearance.
- Tab groups in the Mac's column: an open group is one tinted, rounded surface holding its heading and its tabs, in one of eight colours from Eden's paintings, with an emoji or a symbol of its own or its first tab's site icon. Folded, a group is a heading among your tabs, its name in its colour, its surface coming up under the pointer with a close button; it keeps the tab you are on showing, and folding and unfolding run on a quick spring. A heading is a tab's line exactly, its icon, title and cross where a tab's are, Roomy Tabs and all. Right-click a group for its colours, applied as you pick them, and Rename, Add Icon, New Tab in Group (⌥⌘T), New Group (⌃⌘N), Separate Tabs, Duplicate and Copy All Links. A group lights in its colour while a tab carried over it would join it. Extensions see and set a group's colour as the nearest of the colours their API knows.
- The Mac shows your other devices' tabs in the column: a group for each device under the pins, folded until you open it, with the tabs open there in this space, and a line under them over your own tabs; with icons only, a door in the foot opens every device's list. They were only in History › Tabs on Other Devices before.
- New tabs go to the top of your tabs by default, and the New Tab row stands there too, under the line; with another place chosen in Settings › Tabs it stays at the foot.
- The Mac's pinned tabs grow with Roomy Tabs; the one in front is lit in its site icon's own colour, a glow in from its edge; and a pinned tab holding no page is no longer drawn faded.
- The tab you're on always says its title, as the others do, where it used to say its site until the pointer came onto it, and the setting for that is gone. A page over plain http still says so, an open lock before the title, and so does a site whose name could pass for another's.
- Switching tabs in the Mac's column lights the tab you picked at once, where a grey slid over from the one you left; a long title fades out at its end rather than ending in an ellipsis; and a site whose icon is a few megabytes, its whole artwork, shows it.
- Your tabs on your other devices, carried on with. A page open on your Mac, opened on your iPhone or the other way round, comes up in the space it was open in, as far down as you had read and in reading mode if you were reading it that way; one already open here is switched to, never opened twice. Your other devices' tabs are grouped by space. A new tab offers the page you were just looking at on another device ("Continue from your Mac") and the ones you closed there in the last ten minutes, and the address field suggests pages open on your other devices as you type.
- Spaces and pins on iPhone and iPad, as on the Mac, once Spaces is turned on in Settings: separate sets of tabs, each with its name, icon and colour, signed in where Personal is or with cookies and sign-ins of its own, switched from the tab overview on iPhone and the top of the sidebar on iPad. Pin a page from the menu: it stays at the head of its space, and on the start page; closing it rests it, back at its own page if Return Pins Home is on, and Go Home, Set Current Page as Home, Open as New Tab and Rename are in its menu. A pin can be shown in every space, each with a tab of its own for it. Spaces, pins and their settings sync with the Mac, and an iPhone or iPad that synced before takes over what the Mac had without changing any of it.
- On iPhone, the end of a page scrolls clear of the bar at the bottom, and what a page keeps at its foot — a cookie notice, say — sits above the bar rather than under it. Picking things to hide, the bar takes the address bar's place.
- On the Mac, a pinned row keeps its folder when another device renames or reorders it.
- Hide elements on iPhone and iPad: from the menu, tap what to take off a page, widen it to what holds it if the tap landed inside, and hide it — every time the site opens, as on the Mac, in the same list. A page's links stay put while you pick, and an embedded frame is offered whole. Hidden on This Site shows each thing on the page again before you restore it, and a change reaches every open tab on that site. A private tab's own changes, hiding or restoring, last only as long as it and the windows it opened, and are never kept.
- On the Mac, leaving a page while picking things to hide ends picking, and something the page took away while you pointed at it is never what gets hidden.
- Reading mode on iPhone and iPad: the article alone, in the device's light or dark, from the menu, and the page again on the next one. It is the Mac's reader, now shared, Markdown copies included once turned on in Settings; lead pictures are found on a phone's narrower pages too.
- Hand off pages to your other devices, off until you turn it on in Settings › General: the page in front can be picked up on your iPhone, iPad or another Mac with Handoff, in Eden there or in its browser. Never a private tab's. A page handed over from another device, from Eden there or from its own browser once Eden is your browser, opens in a tab here, whatever the switch says.
- Eden for iPhone and iPad is a browser: tabs and one field for addresses and searches, with suggestions from your tabs, bookmarks and history; private tabs; the Mac's ad and tracker blocker; bookmarks and history kept on the device; a choice of search engine. The field is at the bottom on iPhone, and the tabs down a sidebar on iPad. Handoff works both ways, offering the page in front once it is turned on in Settings.
- A search engine in the list of search engines has its own name again.
- Sync with iCloud, on unless you turn it off in Settings: your open tabs, bookmarks, history, spaces, pins and settings, between your Macs, iPhone and iPad signed in to the same Apple Account, through your own private iCloud database. History › Tabs on Other Devices on the Mac, and On Your Other Devices in the iPhone's tabs and the iPad's sidebar, open what your other devices have open. It replaces syncing through a folder you choose. The iPhone and iPad sync the bookmarks (folders included), history, search engine and blocker, and carry the Mac's spaces and pins along untouched. It runs on Apple's CKSyncEngine, which schedules syncing, retries what didn't reach iCloud and wakes Eden when another device changes something; the Mac now also syncs as tabs open and close. What sync keeps on each device is a SQLite database, written only where it changed, so keeping it up to date takes about a millisecond for a replica of thousands of items, instead of rewriting a file of several megabytes.

  icon, its data folder (`~/Library/Application Support/Eden`), its keychain
  items, its AppleScript dictionary and its own pages (`eden://settings`).

  Updates…, no Updates rows in Settings, nothing fetched (see TODO.md).
